language #217

Hedging Language

Every hedge makes you sound weaker. Just say it.

What it means

People undermine their own authority with hedges like 'I think,' 'I believe,' 'I could be wrong, but,' or 'I don't mean to bother you.' These qualifiers erode credibility before you've made your point. Stating things directly without cushioning makes you sound more confident and clear.

Analogy

Compare 'I think I'd probably be a good fit for this role' versus 'I'm confident I'd be a strong asset to this team.' Same message, completely different level of conviction.

Reflect on this

Record yourself speaking for two minutes on something you care about. Count the hedges ('I think,' 'maybe,' 'I guess'). Re-record without them and notice the shift.
